Privacy Policy for SpaceLens

Last updated: 13 October 2026

SpaceLens is a storage analyzer for Android. It answers one question — what is taking up space on this device — and it answers it on the device itself. This policy explains what the app reads, what it never does, and what the third-party services inside it collect.

SpaceLens is published by Zavi Studio ("we"). It applies to the Android app com.zavistudio.spacelens distributed through Google Play.

The short version

  • There is no account, no sign-in, and no server of ours. We operate no backend for this app.
  • Your files, their names, their folder paths, their thumbnails and their metadata never leave your device, and we never receive them.
  • SpaceLens keeps no index or database of your files. Nothing about them is written to disk — every figure you see is read from Android at the moment it is shown.
  • The app never deletes anything on its own. Deleting always goes through Android's own confirmation, and storage is only reported as freed after Android confirms the file is gone.
  • Advertising and crash reporting are provided by Google and do collect data. Those are the only parts of the app that send anything anywhere.

What SpaceLens reads on your device

To measure your storage, the app queries Android's own system services:

  • MediaStore — Android's media index. Provides file sizes, types, modification dates and folder names for the photos, videos, audio and other files it can see.
  • StorageStatsManager and StatFs — total, used and free space for each storage volume, and, only if you grant Usage access, the combined space used by installed apps and their data.
  • Folders you choose — if you add a folder in Settings → Folder access, the app reads the names, sizes, types and dates of the documents, archives and installers inside that folder, through Android's own document picker.

All of this is read in memory, used to draw what is on screen, and discarded. None of it is transmitted, and none of it is saved.

Permissions, and why each one exists

  • Photos and videos, and music and audio — on Android 13 and above, READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_MEDIA_VIDEO and READ_MEDIA_AUDIO, plus READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED on Android 14 and above; on Android 12L and below, READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE — without these, Android reports almost nothing about your media and the app cannot tell you what is using your space. If you grant only some of them, or only selected items, SpaceLens says so on screen and labels the affected figures as a minimum rather than a total.
  • Modify or delete storage contents (WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, Android 9 and below only) — required to delete a file you have chosen to delete. It is requested at the moment you tap Delete, never at launch. On Android 10 and above it is not requested at all, because your tap in the system's own delete dialog is the permission.
  • Usage access (PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS) — optional, and never requested at launch. It is a special access you switch on yourself in Android's settings. SpaceLens uses it for one thing: asking Android how much space installed apps and their data occupy in total. It does not read which apps you use, when, or for how long, and it does not list your installed apps or send their names anywhere. You can switch it off at any time in Android's settings.
  • Folder access — optional. Android does not let any app see documents, archives or installers created by other apps. If you want those measured, you can pick a folder with Android's own folder picker, and SpaceLens can then read that folder and nothing outside it. The list of folders is kept by Android, not by SpaceLens; you can remove a folder in the app's Settings at any time, and uninstalling the app removes every grant. Files in those folders are deleted only when you select them, review the list and confirm.
  • Internet and network state — used only to request ads and, if enabled, to send crash and usage reports. No feature of the analysis needs the network, and the app works fully offline.

SpaceLens does not request All Files Access (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE). That permission would let the app read every file on the device, and it is not needed to measure storage. The cost of not having it is that some documents, archives and installers placed by other apps are invisible to SpaceLens — and where that happens, the app tells you instead of quietly leaving them out.

What we collect

Nothing, directly. We have no server for this app and receive no data from it. The two Google services embedded in the app collect data under their own policies:

Advertising (Google AdMob)

SpaceLens shows banner advertisements, one native advertisement on the Overview screen (marked “Ad”) and, at most once per session, one full-screen advertisement after a completed delete. Ads are served by Google AdMob, which may collect and process device and usage information — including your device's advertising ID, IP address, and interactions with the ad — to serve and measure advertising. We never send AdMob anything about your files.

  • Google's practices are described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services.
  • If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the app asks for your consent choices before requesting ads, using Google's consent management platform. You can change those choices at any time from Settings → Privacy options inside the app.
  • You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in your device's settings, under Google → Ads.

Crash and usage reporting (Firebase)

If crash reporting is enabled in this build, the app uses Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics, which collect device and app information such as device model, operating system version, app version, an app instance identifier, and — for a crash — a stack trace of the code that failed.

The usage events the app records are deliberately coarse and contain no file information: which screen was opened, which setting was changed, how many files a delete affected and whether it succeeded, and whether an ad was shown. No filename, folder path or file content is ever included in an analytics event or a crash report.

SpaceLens does not offer an in-app switch for usage analytics. Uninstalling the app stops all collection. Firebase's handling of this data is covered by the Firebase privacy documentation and Google's Privacy Policy.

Data stored on your device

SpaceLens saves only your own settings — theme choice, notification preference, your consent choices, and simple counters used to decide when to offer the in-app review prompt. These stay on your device. Uninstalling the app removes them.

The app also offers a "Copy report" button on its Scan details screen, so you can send us a description of a scan when something looks wrong. That report is checked to contain no filenames, paths or names you have typed, and nothing is sent unless you choose to send it.

Deleting files

SpaceLens never deletes a file by itself, and has no "clean all" action. You select files, review the exact list and total, and confirm — through Android's own dialog for photos, videos and audio, and on SpaceLens's review screen for files in a folder you added, which Android deletes permanently with no recycle bin. Afterwards the app verifies with Android which files are actually gone: where it cannot verify, it says so rather than reporting space as freed.

Children

SpaceLens is a general-purpose utility and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Your rights

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to export or erase on request. For the data processed by Google's advertising and analytics services, your choices are the consent form described above, the advertising ID controls in your device settings, and the rights described in Google's own privacy policy.
